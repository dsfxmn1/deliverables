A Multiverse Science-Fiction Thriller

Two Scenes from Fractured Realities

Two people remember a night together that their worlds say never happened, because they are crossing between realities, and the crossings are tearing the multiverse apart.

Book One, As The Sun Rises · on submission to literary agents

Scene One

A Mysterious Visitor

Nadia Rai sits up in bed suddenly, screaming a scream she’s never heard come from herself before. Her eyes are open so wide it hurts, bulging and frantic. She quickly looks all over her room, searching desperately.

“He doesn’t belong... He doesn’t belong... He doesn’t belong,” she repeats over and over, frantically scanning the room with her eyes, moving her head back and forth, searching... for something.

“He doesn’t belong... He doesn’t belong... He doesn’t belong,” she repeats again. Where’s the window? Who took the window? Why can’t I find my window? Her thoughts are frantic and incomprehensible. Her heart beats fast and hard, so hard it feels like it’s going to jump out of her chest.

“He doesn’t belong... He doesn’t belong... He doesn’t belong,” she keeps repeating, now unsure why those words are being repeated. Who is repeating them? That voice. It’s so familiar. It’s her own voice. Why is she saying these words? Unable to stop.

“He doesn’t belong... He doesn’t belong... he...”

Finally, the words stop coming. Her heart begins to slow. With every slow, deep breath she takes, she feels more and more like herself again. The window... there it is, to the right of her bed. The morning sun subtly creeps its way into her bedroom from behind the blackout curtains.

Wait, that’s right, this is her bedroom. She moves her hand from under the bedsheet and the sun crawls across the caramel olive skin on the back of her hand. She sits there staring at the back of her hand for a second, concentrating on the veins, each capillary making its way across her hand like a maze.

She finds the bedside clock on her nightstand. 9 a.m. Sunday morning. Why couldn’t she still be asleep? Who woke her up so early? That woman, screaming. Was it a woman? No... yes... it was herself screaming. Yes, she could recognize the voice.

There’s a knocking... on the wall? No, that’s the door, the door to her bedroom.

“Huh? What’s going on?” she asks herself, half expecting an answer, half just waiting. She’s foggy, her brain in a daze.

“Nadia! Are you okay!?”

A man’s voice comes from the other side of the door. It’s familiar, but she can’t put a face to it. Not yet.

“Dad?” she asks halfheartedly.

“What? No, it’s me Nigel. What’s going on? Are you okay?”

Oh, right, it’s Nigel. Her brother and roommate. They’ve been roommates for most of their adult lives. After their parents died, they had no one else to count on but each other. He’s a very sweet person. Always looking out for her when he can. She couldn’t have asked for a better brother or roommate.

“Nadia, answer me! Are you okay!? You’re scaring me here!” Nigel screams from the other side of the door.

“I’m fine!” she replies. “Hold on!”

Nadia takes one last deep breath, attempting to sigh out the fogginess somehow and return to herself, to her right mind. Inhale deeply, exhale slowly. In through the nose, out through the mouth.

She stands up from bed, not even bothering with her slippers, and almost falls back into bed. She catches herself, palm against the wall, and, looking down at the floor, she shakes her head and tries to blink herself awake.

“Nadia?” Nigel asks through the door once again.

Nadia looks up at the door, almost as if looking at Nigel himself.

“Yeah, I’m coming,” she almost whispers.

Nadia slowly walks over to the door. As she does, she has to concentrate on every step she takes. It’s only a few feet to the door, but it feels like a chasm the size of the Grand Canyon is in her way. Each lumbering step takes more and more effort. As she reaches the door, she turns the knob and pulls, but the knob doesn’t turn, and instead of pulling the door open, she pulls herself into the door.

“Fuck,” she mumbles. “Real smooth, Nadia, you might want to try unlocking the door first.”

Nadia groans in response and punches the door. “Door hard, too morning,” she says sarcastically.

She unlocks the door and opens it. “I’m fine, Nigel, it was just a bad dream.”

“Yeah, I see that now, Nadia. Just one question: Who doesn’t belong?” Nigel asks.

“Uh... him?” Nadia shrugs.

“Rrrrrright. That must’ve been some nightmare. You want some breakfast?” Nigel offers.

Nadia nods and leans on the door frame. “I’ll get ready,” she says as she closes her door.

With great effort, she walks away from her door, past her bed, and opens her curtain, letting the sun in to light her bedroom. She flinches back, scrunching her eyes as they race to contract the pupils in response to the sudden shock of light, bumping into her bed and falling back into it in the process.

“You never want to let me go, huh?” she says to the bed as she runs her hands over the sheets.

She lies there for a moment, almost petting her sheets as the will to get up drains from her.

What a crazy dream.

Then she realizes she doesn’t even know because she can’t remember it.

What was all that about?

Nadia sits up, pushing herself up and out of her sheets. She looks over to the foot of her bed, where she saw... something earlier. There is nothing there now, and most likely, there was nothing there earlier. Just a continuation of her dream that found its way into reality.

Once she has some coffee, this will all be behind her, and she’ll be ready to start her day. She just needs a kickstart. Scrunching her face up again, she lies back down.

“Or I could just stay in bed for the day. There’s no reason I actually have to get up and out on a Sunday,” she says to herself.

Sighing once again, Nadia continues to lie there, drowning in a sea of her thoughts.

Scene Two

The Clock Winds Down

The pencil taps against the desk. An old, spartan desk with a mess of papers all across it. It taps again. The computer’s fan spins up, struggling to keep the heat down. The pencil taps. The old fingers holding it, white fingernail beds squeezing hard. The pencil taps. The loading bar on the screen grows one percent larger, straining with the calculations going on behind the scenes. The pencil taps. The clock ticks and tocks, every movement feeling like it is taking an eternity. The pencil taps, and its holder sucks their teeth with impatience. The pencil taps. The computer’s fan kicks up even harder. It fights a losing battle against the laws of thermodynamics.

The pencil taps, and breaks in two. It is discarded, tossed away inconsequentially. It hits the dirty floor and rolls toward the wall, coming to rest next to a dust bunny. Sunken, dark eyes, with furrowed, furious eyebrows stare into the screen. Unmoving, unflinching, concentrating on that loading bar, which seems to never move. Dry, chapped lips part as gaunt cheeks pull breath into lungs that sound like they’re going to collapse. The harsh fluorescent lighting above reveals the bags under his eyes. He looks like he hasn’t slept in months.

The screen goes blank, the fan dies, and the computer loses power. He lets out a low, deliberate, almost inhuman growl, barely able to contain his rage. Then it is no longer contained. He stands and simultaneously sweeps the computer off the desk with all his might. It flies across the room, violently hitting the wall and breaking into pieces, which scatter across the messy room, a symphony of plastic and metal pieces ringing out throughout the room.

Amid the debris on the floor, he sees a small framed photo. The grimace drains from his face. A younger Cromwell stands in sunlight, arm around a smiling woman; both look enraptured with each other. Crouching, Dr. Cromwell lovingly retrieves the cherished photograph, his expression shifting to one of profound sorrow. Gently, he runs his thumb across the woman’s cheek. Then, just as gently, he puts the photo into his lab coat pocket.

He sits back down and folds his hands on his lap as he rocks in his office chair. He extends his index fingers into a steeple shape and presses them to his salt-and-pepper mustache, the rest of his fingers resting on his thick beard. Deep in thought, his stare only becomes more and more intense. Tension is visibly building in his shoulders. He breathes deeply, methodically. In and out, slowly. A thousand-yard stare into the bare wall in front of him. He strokes his mustache with his index fingers, the beard below his chin with his thumbs. He breathes in and breathes out over and over. Unmoving and unflinching. Stewing in his own anger.

A man in his thirties bursts into the room. He stops in his tracks and looks at the broken computer on the floor. The name tag on his chest reads Gavin Bardot.

“What’s wrong?”

The old man takes his time, responding slowly and deliberately. “Oh, just the Big Bang.”

Gavin looks confused, but doesn’t interrupt.

“Stars burning, planets forming.”

The older man points at himself.

“Evolution.”

The older man turns his chair and makes a gesture toward Gavin.

“Apes learned how to talk.”

Gavin furrows his eyebrows. Rage overcomes the older man’s face.

“About a hundred million sperm, and somehow, you swam the fastest!”

Gavin looks dumbfounded, completely caught off-guard and not knowing what to think.

“My work is everything. Everything you and I, everyone in this office, everyone on this planet, everything that has ever existed. That is how much my work means.”

He slams his fist down on the arm of his chair.

“Knowing all of this, everything that’s riding on this...”

He struggles to stand from his chair.

“Is it so damn much to ask for a computer that’s worth a damn?!”

Gavin stammers, “That—that computer was capable of thirty-two teraflops...”

The older man interrupts, screaming his answer. “I need all the teraflops! Get me something usable!”

He grabs a stapler from his desk and tosses it toward the door where Gavin is standing. It lands by his feet. Gavin rushes out of the room, not even bothering to give an answer. The older man takes his time, guiding himself back into his seat with a groan.

Why do I treat him so badly? I need to control my temper. He’s the one who helped me prove my multiverse theory, after all, he thinks as he turns his seat back to his desk, now empty except for his nameplate, which reads Dr. Avery Cromwell.

In his windowless room, he sits, his hands folded in his lap again. His eyebrows furrow, accentuating every fold and crease already there. He rocks in his chair, starting his intense breathing ritual once again. Breathing in the stale, artificially dry air of the office. Everything smells old. He wonders where his time went. Why he is where he is now.

No sense in dwelling. Only action can help us now, he thinks to himself, looking around at the disgrace of an office he has to deal with.

Tick tock, tick tock, tick tock. The noise of the clock never stops. In his mind, this one is not telling the time. It is counting down to the inevitable. The end.
