Evidence packet

11th Avenue and West 43rd Street:
A Design and Response Failure

Prepared October 19, 2026  ·  For the NYC DOT Manhattan Borough Commissioner's Office, case DOT-751589-X4D5  ·  Source: 144 tracked obstruction reports

Two midtown corridors produce a disproportionate share of New York City's blocked bike lane complaints in this record. Both are painted, unprotected lanes that place riders in the door zone. Neither is failing because drivers are unusually bad there. Both are failing because the street design invites the violation, and because the complaint process arrives hours after the violation has driven away.

The finding. These are not scattered complaints. They cluster on specific blocks, repeat across years, and end the same way: the officer arrives after the vehicle has left, observes nothing, and closes the complaint. That outcome measures response time, not whether a bike lane is blocked.

1. Eleventh Avenue

The lane runs along the parking side, marked by two white lines and bike symbols rather than green paint. That leaves riders inside the door zone with nothing between them and a stopped truck. The reports reflect exactly where the loading pressure is.

2. West 43rd Street

West 43rd presents the same pattern in a shorter span, and it is already the subject of an open DOT case. The block at 670 West 43rd is both the most reported block here and the specific location named in case DOT-751589-X4D5, which DOT's Manhattan Borough Commissioner's office escalated to the Bikes unit for a protected design review.

3. What the outcomes actually say

Across both corridors, the dominant closed outcome is "no action." On 11th Avenue, 16 of 122 reports closed that way. On West 43rd, 7 of 22. Read plainly, the enforcement model is not producing compliance, and it is not designed to. A complaint about a moving, temporary obstruction has a useful life measured in minutes. An officer arriving hours later is measuring something else.

One case illustrates it. On October 18, a yellow box truck was photographed standing inside the lane near 610 West 43rd at 10:15 AM. The report went in through the Reported app. NYPD closed it that evening: no criminal violation observed upon arrival. The address on the complaint came from the photo's GPS and named the corner around the block, but that is not the crux. The crux is that the response came hours later, and the truck was gone. A separate filing went to BLU with the company identified.

4. What we are asking for

  1. Complete the 670 West 43rd Street protected design. Case DOT-751589-X4D5 is already with the Bikes unit. Six reports on that block support finishing it. Give a date for the design review and the installation.
  2. Harden the 610 to 614 block of Eleventh Avenue. These two blocks alone carry 45 reports. Relocate or meter the loading frontage, and add physical separation at the pinch points.
  3. Set a response standard for blocked bike lane complaints. A complaint that is answered hours later cannot be verified. Publish the median response time for these complaint types, by precinct.
  4. Stop counting "no action" as resolution. Report closures separately from verified observations, so the public can see the difference between a lane that was clear and a lane that was never checked in time.

5. Method

Counts are drawn from a continuously maintained log of obstruction reports, one record per report, with dates, blocks, plates and agency outcomes. Where an outcome is pending, it is not counted as a resolution. This packet contains no estimates and no extrapolation. Addresses were corrected where a photo's GPS misreported the location, and the correction is noted in the underlying record.
